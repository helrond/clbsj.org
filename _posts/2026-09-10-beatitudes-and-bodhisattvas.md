Starting in October, CLBSJ will host a bi-monthly interfaith contemplative practice dedicated to exploring scriptures related to moral action as described by Jesus Christ and The Buddha. This bi-monthly gathering of the faithful and the curious will hold space for spiritual practitioners to sit and contemplate together the wisdom of two life-changing texts of ethical and moral guidance: 8 Blessings of the Good Road (The Beatitudes), and 37 Practices of a Bodhisattva. The sessions will convene at 7:00pm Eastern Time on each New Moon and each Full Moon starting on October 10, 2026, and will be led by Sr. Sharifa V. Meytung.

Both written from a place of deep love and compassion for all of creation, these two scriptures hold particular importance for seekers of justice in these urgent times. By engaging them on the new and full moon, we will honor our human connection to nature and its cycles and participate in a process of re-weaving the linkages between our sacred scriptures and indigenous spiritual practices. The new moon sessions will feature readings from The 37 Practices of a Bodhisattva as recorded by the 14th century Buddhist monk, Tokmé Zangpo. The new moon is an auspicious time to focus on these texts, which together provide guidance on how to continually “begin again” at the very basics of ethical living. The full moon sessions will engage Matthew’s version of the Beatitudes as rendered in the First Nations Version (FNV): An Indigenous Bible Translation, in which the Beatitudes are titled “Blessings of the Good Road.” The full moon invites awareness of the fullness of power that is often hidden, and therefore is an auspicious time to focus on the revolutionary ethics outlined in these texts.

“Creator Sets Free (Jesus) lifted up his voice and said to them, ‘I am the light shining on this dark world. The ones who walk with me will not stumble in the darkness but will have the light that gives them life.’” ~ The Gospel According to John 8:12 (FNV)

“A Bodhisattva should not learn many doctrines. If a Bodhisattva thoroughly upholds and thoroughly understands one doctrine, all the qualities of a Buddha are in the palm of his hand. What is that one doctrine? Great compassion.”  ~ Dharmasamgiti Sutra
